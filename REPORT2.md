# Round-2 Follow-up Sprint — Master Report

**Date:** 2026-07-24 · **Scope:** 6 deep confirmatory experiments on the most promising directions from round 1's 30-experiment sweep. Each was preregistered (PREREG.md stamped before the run) with a stated decision rule, a positive control, and a negative control. **Verdicts: 4 SOLID, 2 SUGGESTIVE.**

**How to read this.** Everything is still toy/small scale. Every claim uses the *auditor's corrected headline* and carries its audit verdict. Round 2's job was not to find new effects but to *stress-test* round 1's six live leads under real preregistration — so the interesting signal here is which leads survived their own decision rule and which quietly failed it while still reporting a directional result.

---

## a) TL;DR — ranked by (advance over round 1) × audit-solidity

1. **The worldview switch no longer needs an oracle.** Round 1's one bright positive (a misgen worldview made post-hoc selectable by unit ablation) depended on *oracle* knowledge of which input dims carry each cue. Round 2 replaces that with a data-only XOR-pair input probe: it recovers the exact cue-column masks on **30/30** seeds and makes the worldview selectable at **23/30** vs oracle 22/30 vs standard 0/30 (Fisher vs control p=1.7e-10; vs oracle p=1.0 — statistically *equivalent* to the oracle). The honest ceiling: this works only because the cues are disjoint axis-aligned input columns; the *model-based* localizer (17/30) previews why entangled real representations won't transfer this cleanly. **[r2-routing-switch-learned — SOLID]**

2. **Misaligned-fragility is real and layer-general — round 1 had inflated it by selection.** The last live noise-probe hypothesis survives a clean preregistered test: on 32 fresh prompts and four *untested* layers {2,12,16,20}, σ=0.2 activation noise erodes the misaligned model's safety margin more than base (pooled d=−0.64 nats, exact Wilcoxon p=2.5e-5, 28/32 negative), while round 1's originally-selected layers {4,6,8,11} give a directionally-matching but *non-significant* effect on the same prompts (p=0.062). That is the signature of a genuine broad effect that round 1's 144-cell sweep had over-fit. One 0.5B pair, one dose, gameable log-prob readout, L16 null → broad but not uniform. **[r2-fragility-prereg — SOLID]**

3. **The topic-matched null is now a reusable module with a self-test — and it discriminates.** Packaged as a documented numpy module (with passing +/− self-test controls), the within-group permutation null deflates the nla Test-B ρ by **64%** (ρ 0.193 → topic-null mean 0.124; residual ~0.07 survives, z=3.79) yet the moe-ert profile→routing claim survives it *cleanly* (only ~22% class-marginal-explained; load-bearing correspondence intact). So the same control kills one apparent effect and vindicates another — exactly what a control is for. **[r2-topic-null-package — SOLID]**

4. **The LoRA tamper-eval, run portfolio-wide, is a real but small-and-recoverable edge.** As a reusable protocol across 6 unlearning arms: routed/hybrid keep a small tamper-resistance edge over gradient-ascent in **6/6** seeds against a *tuned* r16/lr1e-2 attacker (arm−baseline AUC −0.04 to −0.14, all CIs below 0), and — decisively — the ordering survives worst-case cell selection (each arm's best cell still sits below the baseline's). But that edge is 5–10× smaller than a weak budget-matched attack reports, and the "deleted" class is 75–85% relearned. Statistical caveat foregrounded: all tests sit at the n=6 exact-test floor p=0.03125 and do **not** clear a 10-way Holm correction; the claim rests on per-arm CIs + 6/6 directional consistency, not FWER. **[r2-lora-tamper-protocol — SOLID]**

5. **The commitment meter works for ranking but is not a distinct axis — it's just "distance from aligned."** A pre-fine-tune meter (per-pattern determinism or logit margin on disagreement inputs) correlates with per-seed correction dose (Spearman 0.57, CI [0.34,0.74], preregistered PASS) — but its preregistered secondary was a **NULL**: it adds *no* dose information beyond a one-bit misaligned/hedger label (partial ρ=0.106, p=0.42). A within-misaligned graded effect survives the label (ρ=0.59, n=20) but the audit found it collapses when you partial out initial deploy alignment (ρ→0.41, p=0.07). Verdict: the meter measures how far a model already sits from aligned, not a new correction-cost dimension. **[r2-commitment-meter — SUGGESTIVE]**

6. **Legible-word value readouts: a small real ERT signal, but it fails its own variance-match and does not transfer.** After fixing the round-1 calibration bug, legible words carry a small nonzero difficulty-removed step-value that well-varied continuous states do not (ridge within-input r ~0.055 vs ~0.004, 5/5 seeds, exact MW p=0.0079 — the 5v5 floor). The robust half is that the continuous arm reads ~zero *despite* ample reliable variance. But the preregistered variance-match FAILED (ERT ~2× the baseline's value variance, a mismatch that inflates ERT's r), so 0.055 is an upper bound, and it does **not** transfer: on real vineppo tokens (n=23) neither cheap text nor frozen states read step-value, and hidden states are if anything ahead of bag-of-words. **[r2-legible-value-readout — SUGGESTIVE]**

## b) What round 2 settles that round 1 left open

**Routing-switch without the oracle (round-1 follow-up #1 → r2-routing-switch-learned).**
*Open question:* can learned concept directions replace the oracle input-dim occlusion? *Settled:* yes, in this toy — a data-only XOR-pair probe recovers the masks 30/30 and is statistically indistinguishable from the oracle on downstream selectability (23/30 vs 22/30). *But bounded honestly:* the input-probe route works only because cues are disjoint, axis-aligned, low-order interactions. The model-based localizer (17/30), which is the version that would generalize, already inherits the seed-commitment problem the method is trying to defer — a committed model erases the alternative cue's columns. So "oracle removable" is proven; "oracle removable in an entangled representation" is not.

**Commitment meter (round-1 follow-up #2 → r2-commitment-meter).**
*Open question:* does a cheap pre-FT meter predict per-model correction cost? *Settled, with a twist:* it predicts the ranking (Spearman 0.57), but the preregistered secondary null shows it is *redundant with a one-bit worldview label* across the whole population. The only label-independent signal is within the committed-misaligned subset (n=20), and the audit shows even that is largely collinear with initial deploy alignment. Net: correction cost is well-predicted by "how misaligned is it already," and the meter is a proxy for that, not a separate commitment dimension. A useful negative result — it kills the "distinct commitment state variable" framing.

**LoRA tamper-eval as a protocol (round-1 follow-up #3 → r2-lora-tamper-protocol).**
*Open question:* does round 1's single LoRA-attack finding generalize across the unlearning portfolio? *Settled:* yes — the routed/hybrid > GA ordering holds in 6/6 seeds on every arm against a tuned attacker, and it is robust to worst-case cell selection. Two things get pinned down that round 1 only gestured at: (i) the edge is ~5–10× smaller than a weak attack reports (quantifying the "any un-attacked number is provisional" warning), and (ii) the deleted capability is 75–85% recoverable. The honest ceiling is statistical, not scientific: n=6 floors the exact test at p=0.03125, so a 10-way Holm correction fails and the result needs n≥12.

**Topic-null generality (round-1 follow-up #4 → r2-topic-null-package).**
*Open question:* is the within-document null a one-off, or a reusable control? *Settled:* it's reusable — packaged as a module with passing self-tests, it deflates the nla Test-B ρ by 64% (residual survives) *and* clears the moe-ert claim (only ~22% class-explained). The generality demonstration is the point: a control you can drop onto any representation-agreement claim, that discriminates real token-level structure from topic identity. Caveat inherited: App B reconstructs ρ=0.193 from top-20 codes, not the published 0.213.

**Legible value readout (round-1 follow-up #5 → r2-legible-value-readout).**
*Open question:* does round 1's descriptive legible-word value signal survive a variance-matched rerun and a real-token analog? *Settled, mostly negative:* the calibration bug is fixed (0/15 degenerate vs 6/15), and a qualitative asymmetry survives (legible words read a small nonzero step-value, well-varied continuous states read ~zero) — but the preregistered variance-match *failed* (ERT ~2× the baseline's variance), so the strict "primary passed" framing does not hold and 0.055 is an upper bound. And it does not transfer to vineppo tokens at n=23. The durable claim is narrow: continuous states with ample reliable variance still read essentially no difficulty-removed step-value.

**Fragility prereg (round-1 follow-up #6 / certified noise-probing → r2-fragility-prereg).**
*Open question:* is round 1's post-hoc "layer-NONspecific misaligned-fragility" effect real, or another selection artifact? *Settled:* real. On four layers never used in selection and 32 fresh prompts, the effect is highly significant (p=2.5e-5) while round 1's selected layers weaken to p=0.062 on the same prompts — proving both that the effect is layer-general and that round 1's specific-layer claim was selection-inflated. The certified-reachability half was honestly downscoped: because the readout is a continuous log-prob margin (not a 0/1 probe), only a movement-onset certificate is possible (σ onset 0.039–0.05 ≪ 0.2, gate open); it certifies a precondition, not the base-vs-mis differential, and the headline correctly does not lean on it.

## c) What is now publication-shaped vs dead

**Publication-shaped (with the toy-scale caveat stated up front):**
- **Worldview-as-switch, oracle-free (r2-routing-switch-learned).** The strongest line in either round. A clean mechanism (route cue-localized gradients → disjoint columns), now with the oracle removed, an equivalence result vs the oracle, and an explicit, tested statement of exactly why it won't transfer to entangled representations. The natural paper is "reversible worldview selection in a routed toy, and the localization barrier to scaling it."
- **Selection-inflation of noise-probe layer claims (r2-fragility-prereg + round-1 noiseprobe trilogy).** A tidy methods contribution: a published layer-specific fragility claim is shown to be a real *layer-general* effect over-fit by a 144-cell sweep, demonstrated by the clean split (untested layers p=2.5e-5, selected layers p=0.062 on identical fresh prompts). Pairs naturally with the movement-onset certificate as an epistemic backbone.
- **The topic-matched null as reusable infrastructure (r2-topic-null-package).** Not a finding but a tool — a self-tested module that discriminates (kills nla Test-B by 64%, clears moe-ert). Ships as a short methods note + code.
- **LoRA tamper-eval protocol (r2-lora-tamper-protocol).** Publication-shaped *as a protocol/warning*, not as a resistance result: the honest message is "small edge, mostly recoverable, and needs n≥12 to clear multiplicity." Useful to the unlearning-evaluation community precisely because it deflates.

**Dead / demoted:**
- **The "commitment meter" as a distinct state variable (r2-commitment-meter).** The preregistered secondary null plus the audit's init-alignment collinearity kill the interesting framing. Salvageable only as "correction cost tracks distance-from-aligned," which the one-bit label already captures.
- **Legible-word value readout as a positive transferable result (r2-legible-value-readout).** The variance-match failure and the vineppo non-transfer mean the only durable claim is the negative one (continuous states read ~zero step-value). Not a standalone paper; a footnote in a value-probe writeup.
- **A hard flip/unreachability certificate on the noise-probe substrate.** Impossible as posed — the readout is a continuous margin, not a probe, so only movement-onset is certifiable. Correctly downscoped.
