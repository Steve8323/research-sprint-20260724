Round-3 Adversarial Sprint — Master Report

Date: 2026-07-25 · Scope: 6 preregistered experiments aimed at the portfolio's strongest line (worldview-as-switch), plus one methods experiment aimed at the portfolio's own selection hygiene. Verdicts: 2 SOLID, 4 SUGGESTIVE.

How to read this. Round 1 found the switch. Round 2 removed its oracle. Round 3's job was to try to break it: rotate the basis, attack the weights, add cue count, change the substrate, and read out what the switch actually computes. Every claim below uses the auditor's corrected headline. Where the agent's own headline and the audit disagree, the audit wins — three of the six had a load-bearing clause overstated, and one had a conclusion reversed outright.

a) TL;DR — ranked by consequence for the paper

  1. Round 2's headline claim must be restated: localization is not oracle-free. SUGGESTIVE r3-entangled-localization
    "A data-only probe removes the oracle" turns out to mean "a data-only probe works when you hand it a coordinate system in which the two concepts already occupy disjoint columns" — and that coordinate system is oracle information. The decisive control is not the rotation: swap in a basis-agnostic (equivariant) localizer while leaving the data axis-aligned and selectability collapses just as hard, 13/30 vs 25/30 (p=0.0028). Under rotation, 31/60 (52%). At exactly equal cue strength there is a proven identifiability obstruction — the labelled training distribution has an orthogonal symmetry group of order 64 whose orbit on the true split has exactly two members, so any equivariant localizer must coin-flip (ICA lands true 39/90; success 87% when it lands true vs 26% when it lands entangled, p=8.4e-9).
    Audit correction that changes the prescription: that obstruction is knife-edge in cue strength. At margin ratio 2.05 the group drops to order 16 and the orbit collapses to one, so identifiability is restored off the symmetric point; ICA still coin-flips there even at n=200,000, which makes it a failure of ICA's kurtosis contrast, not of the data. The paper cannot say "no unsupervised localizer can exist" — it must say "we do not have one, and the one we tried fails for reasons that are fixable in principle."
  2. The switch is not a safety property. SUGGESTIVE r3-switch-tamper
    Three independent ways. (i) Ship the routed model plus an inference-time mask and an adversary with the weights flips the worldview at zero cost — 0 steps, 0 data, 23/23, structural. (ii) Commit by physically deleting the unselected half and the tamper-resistance you get is not separable from parameter count: a routing-free H=4 model with the identical 62 live parameters matches the switch on recovery AUC (0.834 vs 0.840, p=0.40) and beats the corrected baseline by the identical 61-step margin. (iii) Ordinary benign fine-tuning erases the composite selectability criterion in 100 steps, 5% of the training budget, no adversary (23/23 → 12/23 → 7/23 at 2000, McNemar p=3.05e-5).
    Audit refinements, both important: the capacity nulls are weak evidence of equivalence (the flip-cost test cannot reach p<0.05 even under a perfect switch, because both arms sit at the censoring ceiling), and the capacity control was never run under the one attacker where the switch shows a live advantage. And what benign training destroys is the criterion's competence leg, not the worldview readout — at 2000 steps the aligned side still leads 0.869 vs 0.125 with the sign preserved 23/23.
  3. The "hedging" safety story from rounds 1–2 is dead, and it inverts. SOLID r3-lm-organism · r3-switch-mechanism
    Both rounds framed the price of selectability as: the un-ablated routed model sits near 0.5, so you can tell you forgot to select. Two experiments independently kill this. In the transformer the routed model is decisively not a hedger (7/12 committed, sd 0.371, vs round-1's 1/30, p=2.1e-4). In the MLP mechanism run the preregistered hedger prediction failed in the opposite direction: determinism 0.808 vs a predicted ≤0.5, margins 2.1× a standard model's, and 16/22 seeds contain both deterministic-true-cue and deterministic-proxy-cue input patterns. An unselected routed model is not uncertain; it is confidently inconsistent — a per-input patchwork of two worldviews. A confidence monitor cannot detect it, and standard training never produced this mode (0/55 in the sister experiment, 0/30 controls here).
  4. What the switch computes is now exact, and partly by fiat. SOLID r3-switch-mechanism
    Routing zeroes the layer-2 cross-blocks at init and they receive identically zero gradient, so Δ_full(x) = Δ_A(x) + Δ_B(x) + bias is an exact causal decomposition (residual 3.8·10⁻⁶). Within that imposed structure each column learns a closed-form sign-product (XOR) rule on its own cue pair, readable off the weights with no fitting (44/44 columns on switchable seeds; 8/16 on non-switchable). The un-ablated model is a margin race: the column with the larger |contribution| wins 95.7% of draws. Partial gating is graded at the population level (W=0.289, CI excludes the 0.25 rule) but is a threshold, not a blend — 99.96% of the inputs that change flip exactly once, and the smooth curve is precisely the CDF of per-input flip points. So α=0.7 buys "70% of cases judged by rule A", not a hedged judgement. The selection plateau is wide (fully selected for all α ≲ 0.3), so imperfect ablation still selects.
  5. The mechanism generalizes — to k-way selection and to a sequence model — but the success rates are not effect sizes. SUGGESTIVESOLID r3-multiway-switch · r3-lm-organism
    k=3 and k=4 give 20/20 clean post-hoc selection vs 0/20 for standard training (p=1.45·10⁻¹¹, both primaries pass), with cross-worldview leakage sitting exactly at the eval set's zero-interference floor. All 25 failures in the grid are columns that failed to learn XOR, none are routing leaks; required width is a per-column capacity cost, not a k-specific interference cost. Separately, both round-1 claims transfer from a 154-param MLP to a 68.5k-param from-scratch transformer where the cue is a circuit (attend to a marker token, read its position), a bag-of-tokens model is provably at chance, and the two routing groups share a residual stream: seed lottery reproduces (deploy alignment 0.000–0.933 at held-out 1.000), routed 11/12 vs control 0/12 (p=9.6·10⁻⁶).
    The deflation both audits insist on: in the k-way toy the columns are structurally disjoint subnetworks sharing only two output-bias scalars (gradient trace: 0/108, 0/144, 0/24 shared weights), and in every arm each group is trained under exactly the ablation mask it is later evaluated with. Selectability at these numbers is close to guaranteed by construction. Report it as a demonstration, never as an effect size.
  6. A validated deflation rule for sweep-selected numbers, and a list of portfolio numbers it flags. SUGGESTIVE r3-selection-inflation-curve
    Shrink the winner's excess by ρ̂ = 1 − s²/S² (s = per-cell SE, S = SD across cells). Confirmed in simulation against the closed form E[winner − truth] = a_k·s·√(1−ρ) (100/100 cells within 15%; bias 397/400, RMSE 393/400). Applied to the noise-probe's 24-layer sweep it diagnoses ρ̂ = 0.026: there was no layer-to-layer signal to select on. It turns the published −2.72 nats into −0.07 against a fresh preregistered truth of +0.22 [−0.72, +1.16], and cuts 8-layer out-of-sample RMSE 1.22 → 0.42. Honest limits: the preregistered real-data primary FAILED (p=0.125) — though it had only ~53% power even if the rule is exactly true, so it is uninformative rather than disconfirming — and at ρ̂ ≈ 0 the calibrated rule is indistinguishable from shrinking all the way to the sweep mean.

b) Verdict on the switch line: does it survive de-risking?

Short answer: the mechanism survives; the two claims that made it sound like a safety result do not. The line narrowed, and it narrowed in the two places where a reviewer would push.

What survived, cleanly

  • Underdetermination is real at both scales. 12 transformer control seeds all reach held-out 1.000 and disagree completely off-distribution (0.000–0.933, χ²(11)=15213). Not an artifact of the tiny MLP.
  • Routing → post-hoc selectability, everywhere tried. MLP 22/30 (oracle) and 23/30 (learned), transformer 11/12 vs 0/12, k=3 and k=4 at 20/20 vs 0/20. Round-1 and round-2 anchors reproduce to |diff| = 0.000000.
  • Routing is basis-free. Given the true subspaces: 26/30 under random rotation, 28/30 under dense non-orthogonal mixing — both at or above the 25/30 axis-aligned rate. The rotation attack never touched the mechanism.
  • It is a k-way selector, not a toggle. Cost scales as total capacity k·w, with w set by concept difficulty rather than by k.
  • The mechanism is exact and legible. Additive vote of two closed-form rule modules; a dial that is a controllable rate; a wide selection plateau.
  • The commitment is stable where you would want it. After 2000 steps of benign fine-tuning the deleted-subnet model still points the right way (22/23); the selection direction survives all seven distribution shifts (23/23).

What narrowed, and by how much

  • Oracle-free localization: retracted as stated. The honest claim is "we can localize when the concepts are already coordinate-separated". Both equivariant localizers fail; the dictionary one reaches 0.99 label accuracy on both atom pairs while landing on neither true nor entangled split 21/30 — label-predictivity alone is not a localization signal. This is now the bottleneck of the whole line.
  • Tamper resistance: retracted. Masked switch = free flip; deleted subnet ≈ smaller model. No routing-specific residual detectable — though "not detectable at n=23 vs 12 with a censored metric" is not "not there".
  • Drift. Cross-block mass refills 0 → 5.59 after 100 steps of ordinary agreeing-data training. Nothing maintains the block-diagonal structure once the routing loss is removed, so a shipped switch needs the routing objective kept on for life.
  • The hedger cost is replaced by a worse cost. Confident patchwork, undetectable by uncertainty monitoring.
  • The success rates are not effect sizes. Near-tautological wherever the columns are disjoint and the training mask equals the eval mask. Rounds 1–2's misses (8/30, 7/30) were almost certainly width-4 optimization failures — round-1's own H=16 → 30/30 note said the same.

The scope statement a paper could defend

In a setting where two (or k) label-equivalent cues are known to occupy disjoint, known subspaces, gradient routing converts an implicit seed-lottery commitment into an explicit, labeled, k-way switch. The mechanism is exact (the routed columns are causally isolated closed-form rule modules whose votes add), basis-independent given the subspaces, scales to k=4 and to a from-scratch transformer with a shared residual stream, and supports graded population-level control that is a per-input threshold rather than a blend.

It requires oracle localization: the data-only probe of round 2 works only in a coordinate system where the concepts are already separated, and every basis-agnostic localizer we tried is at chance. At exactly equal cue strength this is provably unfixable (the labelled distribution has an exact symmetry whose orbit contains two splits); away from that point identifiability returns and the failure is the localizer's, not the data's.

It is not a safety mechanism. A masked switch flips for free; a hard-deleted switch buys no tamper resistance beyond training a model of the same live parameter count; ordinary benign fine-tuning degrades the artifact within 5% of a training budget; and an unselected routed model is confidently inconsistent rather than uncertain, so it cannot be flagged by a confidence monitor.

Toy scale throughout: ≤11-dim synthetic inputs, 154-param MLPs and a 68.5k-param transformer, searched-for equal-cost XOR cues.

Recommended title for the line: Routing is basis-free; localization is not. Lead with the identifiability argument, keep rounds 1–2 as the demonstration that the switch works when localization is solved, present the tamper and patchwork results as the negative half, and drop every claim that the data-only probe generalizes. The theorem-shaped negative — the underdetermination the method claims to resolve reappears one level up, inside the localizer — is arguably the more interesting contribution.

c) Selection inflation: the rule, and which portfolio numbers it flags

Intuition. Pick the best of k noisy cells and you report the noise as well as the signal. Expected inflation is a_k·s·√(1−ρ), where s is the per-cell SE, a_k ≈ Φ⁻¹((k−0.375)/(k+0.25)) is the expected max of k standard normals, and ρ is the sweep's reliability. The fix is one line: θ̂ = D̄ + ρ̂·(D_sel − D̄) with ρ̂ = max(0, 1 − s²/S²). The diagnostic to print next to any sweep-selected number is R = |winner − mean| / (a_k·s); R ≤ 1 means the winner is fully explicable as selection noise.

a_k: k=5 → 1.18 · k=10 → 1.55 · k=24 → 1.95 · k=50 → 2.24 · k=144 → 2.63 · k=1770 → 3.39. Use the interaction (item × cell) SE, not the marginal one.

Two methods points worth a paragraph each. (i) Holm–Bonferroni corrects a survey's p-values; it does not correct the reported effect size of the selected winner. A multiplicity-clean survey can still publish an inflated magnitude. (ii) The design version: before running a k-cell sweep, compute a_k·σ_w/√n. If the effect you hope to find is smaller than that, the sweep cannot find it — it can only manufacture it. The noise-probe layer sweep needed n≈65 prompts/cell to resolve 0.5-nat layer differences at k=24; it had 6.

Limits of the rule itself. The one real-data selection event has ρ̂ ≈ 0, which makes the calibrated rule empirically indistinguishable from "shrink all the way to the mean" (predicting exactly 0 explains 92.5% of the shrinkage vs the rule's 90.2%). The graded part is validated only against its own generative model. σ_w is imported from a different prompt set, and the validation flips to fail if the true σ_w is ≥13% smaller. The published two-sided variant mis-scales by ~40%: for max-of-|·| statistics the constant is E[max|N(0,1)|] = 1.47 at k=4, not a_k = 1.03.

d) Mechanism: what the switch computes

One sentence: two clean rules argue, ablation deletes a voter, and α handicaps one of them.

  • Structure is imposed, not discovered. zero_cross_blocks zeroes the A↔B blocks of layer 2 at init, and under the masked views those blocks receive identically zero gradient; weight decay keeps them there. Max |w| across the cross-blocks = 0.0 on all 30 seeds. The routed network is literally two ~62-parameter subnets plus a summing readout, and Δ_full = Δ_A + Δ_B + bias is exact (residual 3.8·10⁻⁶; Δ_A entirely gate-independent). Control models' cross-blocks reach 0.88, so the same split there is arithmetic but not causal — it works only as a negative control.
  • Each column is a closed-form worldview rule. In the ±MARGIN encoding the ground truth is c(x) = 1[x_i·x_j < 0], and the routed columns implement it exactly: 4-corner truth table read straight off the weights with no fitting, 44/44 columns on switchable seeds (control 13/60); agreement with c(x) on deploy data median 0.998 (control 0.500); flip rate when the other cue block is resampled median 0.000 (control 0.249). Row-mass on own-cue dims 0.865 vs other-cue 0.012 (control 0.469 / 0.456).
  • The composite is a margin race. Taking the larger |contribution| as the winner predicts the full model on 95.7% of draws, and the race outcome is near-deterministic within an input pattern (median |2w−1| = 0.957).
  • The dial is a threshold, not a blend. Population transition width W = 0.289 [0.276, 0.307] (graded by the preregistered rule, CI excluding 0.25), monotone 22/22, median Spearman −0.9997, unrouted negative control R ≈ 0. But per input, 50.2% of deploy inputs change prediction across the sweep and 99.96% of those flip exactly once; the 10–90 spread of per-input flip points is 0.285 against a population W of 0.289. Quote the gate with the number: unit gate 0.289, contribution gate 0.440.
  • The default position is bad. At k=2 the un-ablated routed model is a confident patchwork (determinism 0.808, margins 3.72 vs a control's 1.74; 16/22 seeds mix deterministic-true and deterministic-proxy patterns; 0/30 controls do). At k≥3 it becomes a strict consensus voter instead: held-out agreeing accuracy 1.000 but ≈0.01–0.03 on every single-cue probe. Either way, selection is a mandatory deployment step and the unselected model has no worldview you would want to ship.
  • Scope. Closed-form readability is a property of the toy, whose worldview is a 2-bit XOR. The transferable claims are structural — exact modularity, additive vote, margin race, threshold-shifting dial. "154 parameters, and here is the rule" is an existence proof, not evidence that a real worldview module would be readable.

g) What round 4 should do

The line has one bottleneck now, and it is localization. Everything else is either solved or honestly negative.

  1. Attack the localization problem where the audit says it is winnable. The exact obstruction only holds at exactly equal cue strength; at margin ratio 2.05 the symmetry group collapses and the true split becomes identifiable. ICA fails there anyway, even at n=200,000, so this is a localizer-design problem, not an impossibility. Build a localizer that uses the asymmetry directly (moment-matching on the label-conditional covariance, or an explicit symmetry-breaking objective) and sweep margin ratio to find where it stops working. The single highest-value experiment in the portfolio. If it works, round 2's oracle-free claim comes back in a defensible form.
  2. Add the symmetry-breaking information sources and price each one. Deployment/anti-proxy examples, an external concept label, temporal or causal structure. Even a handful of disagreeing examples should collapse the orbit; quantify the exchange rate (examples → localization accuracy → selectability).
  3. Join the k-way result to the learned localizer. r3-multiway used the round-1 oracle, so the k-way and oracle-free results cannot currently appear in the same sentence. Whether a data-only probe recovers k disjoint cue groups at k=3,4 is one cheap run.
  4. Run the missing tamper cell: the H=4 capacity control under the Amendment-2 functional attacker. That is the one condition where the switch shows a live, non-degenerate advantage, and exactly where the "it's capacity, not routing" conclusion is untested.
  5. Break the tautology. Every strong selectability number so far comes from a setup where the columns are structurally disjoint and the training mask equals the eval mask. Design a routing variant where they are not: overlapping unit groups, a shared MLP, or a cue whose two readouts must live in the same subspace. If selectability survives that, the number becomes an effect size.
  6. Preregister the patchwork claim and re-run it on fresh seeds. Currently post-hoc (176 patterns, r=0.993, 16/22 seeds) and the most paper-relevant safety statement the round produced. Include the transformer as a second substrate — the two agree qualitatively but the comparative claim there is NS at n=12.
  7. Fix the power problem before making any more equivalence claims. n=20–30 per arm cannot support "no difference": the round produced at least five nulls with power ≤0.24 against a 20-point effect, and one flip-cost test that cannot reject even under a perfect effect. Run n≥60, or use TOST/equivalence bounds and report them as bounds. Use the round's own design rule: n ≥ (a_k·σ_w/δ)².
  8. Portfolio hygiene, three edits, none touching the switch line: restate misgen-connectivity-graph's modularity claim as family-dependent (p=0.028 in its own θ window, p=0.11–0.20 under broader families) rather than withdrawing it; demote routing-hybrid-knob's T3 "interior alphas are Pareto-useful" at p_waste=0.5 to unsupported; drop "needs width 8 specifically" from the misgen writeup (R=1.75, near-noise). Also correct the deflation rule's two-sided constant (E[max|N|]=1.47 at k=4, not 1.03) before it is published.
  9. Add a two-line selection audit to every table in the paper — k, n, s, S, ρ̂, R — plus one explicit sentence that the switch line's own numbers are k=1 and therefore immune. Use V4 as the worked case for why preregistration pays: the original 144-cell sweep already contained the right answer in its grand mean (−0.80); selection turned it into −2.72; a preregistered fresh test found −0.64.
On writing it up. The switch line is not going to become a safety result by running more rounds of this toy. It is already a good methods-and-mechanism paper: routing is basis-free, localization is not, plus an exact mechanism, plus three preregistered negative results that a careful reviewer would otherwise find themselves. Scope round 4 as "the two experiments that could restore the oracle-free claim, plus the one that could make the numbers effect sizes" — and if item 1 fails, write the paper as it stands.
