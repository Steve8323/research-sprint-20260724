## a) TL;DR — ranked by consequence for the paper

1. **Round 4's reframe is a width law, and round 5 measures the law.** Re-scoring *every* routing-free control in the line under one identical data-chosen-mask procedure (FMP, verified bit-identical to round 4's: max|diff| = 0.0, masks identical 10/10), the pooled floor moves **2/282 → 100/282** — but the movement is almost entirely in cells whose ablation groups are **8 units wide**: **88/120 (73.3 %) at w = 8 vs 12/150 (8.0 %) at w = 4** (Fisher p = 2.5·10⁻³⁰, POST-HOC). Of 13 confirmatory cells: **9 SURVIVE, 2 SHRINK, 2 DIE**. Round 4's +0.150 is a w = 8 number. At w = 4 — the width rounds 1 and 2 actually used — routing's corrected contribution is **+0.667 to +0.700**, and in the transformer the floor **does not move at all** (0/12 → 0/12, effect stays +1.000). **[r5-fair-floor-propagation — SOLID]**

2. **The mechanism behind the width law is per-half competence, and it dissociates cleanly from basis.** In the MLP the latent switch appears at 2/30 → 25/30 → 29/30 → 30/30 for H = 8/16/32/64 (Cochran–Armitage z = 7.97, p = 7.8·10⁻¹⁶). But the gate is not selectability, it is **whether each data-chosen half individually clears the 0.9 held-out bar**: both halves are competent in 5/26/29/30 of 30 seeds, and *conditional on both being competent* the mask selects in **2/5, 25/26, 29/29, 30/30**. In the round-3 transformer the halves **are** competent (held-out 1.000/1.000 on both sides, 12/12 in the post-hoc probe) and the mask still selects **0/30** — Wilson upper 0.114 at power 0.87 against a true rate of 0.60, and the auditor's added positive control shows the instrument works (the same mask-finder scores 4/4 with head recovery 1.00 on routed transformers). **So there are two independent conditions, and width satisfies only one of them.** **[r5-latent-switch-standard — SUGGESTIVE]**

3. **The exchange rate — the result that decided round 4's framing — does not hold as stated.** The routing arm was scored two-sidedly (shipped subnet aligned AND the other subnet anti-aligned AND both held-out > 0.9) while augmentation had only to deliver one aligned model. Rescored on the matched outcome — the label-designated subnet you would actually ship — routing gives **16/21/25/25/28 of 30** at m = 1/2/4/8/32 against augmentation's **14/16/24/30/30**. The m = 8 gap falls from Fisher p = 0.0046 to **0.052** (paired McNemar 0.063) and m = 32 from 0.024 to **0.49**; m = 32 fails the preregistered Bonferroni α = 0.0083 either way. **"Routing never beats augmentation at any price tested" and "routing is worse at m = 8 and m = 32" are both false.** What survives is weaker and still decisive enough: augmentation reaches a ceiling routing does not (30/30, deploy alignment 0.96–0.99 from m ≥ 8, vs routing's best 28/30). **[r4-symmetry-breaking-exchange — SUGGESTIVE]**

4. **Optionality wins exactly one regime out of four, and that regime is a transport result, not a creation result.** NO-RETRAIN: routing 60/60 is **equivalent** to ordinary training plus a no-gradient intervention (59/60; TOST at δ = 0.15 passes with 90 % CI [0.000, 0.050]). UNKNOWN-TARGET: routing **loses** — 100 fine-tuning steps per change match it 240/240 seed-for-seed at ~60 % of the cost, and ordinary-training-plus-mask switches target 234/240 with *zero* gradient steps at half the training cost. UNLABELLED-ONLY: nobody wins, by equivariance. **BLIND** (deployment actor has no cue oracle): routing wins decisively, 60/60 from two labels onward vs 4/60, against a fair 200-mask search that saturates near 51–54/60 even at m = 32. The honest reading, which the agent states itself: routing **records the training-time cue oracle in the architecture** for an actor who no longer has it. **[r5-optionality-case — SOLID]**

5. **"Benign fine-tuning erases the switch" was the same artifact, and it is now falsified.** Round 4's preregistered secondary said 0/30 fine-tuned models remain switchable. Under a data-chosen mask, **44/60 (73 %, [0.610, 0.829]) are still switchable**. What benign fine-tuning destroys is the *labelling*, not the switch. This weakens the strongest single argument REPORT3/4 had against the switch as a shipped artifact — and it cuts *against* our own safety-negative, so it must be stated plainly. **[r5-optionality-case — SOLID]**

6. **The tamper table's numbers all reconcile; two of its interpretations do not.** (i) The "dynamic-range" ground for withdrawing round 3's flip-cost equivalence is **false**: the check was mis-implemented as `mwu([101]*n, [101]*n)` — both arms pinned at the censoring bound, i.e. the exact null configuration. The preregistered configuration through the same helper returns p ≈ 5·10⁻²⁴, and round 3's own h4-vs-corrected flip test rejected at p = 0.0007 on identically censored data. The correct ground for withdrawal is **low power** (n = 23 v 12, 16/23 and 11/12 tied at the bound). (ii) **C6 is not capacity-matched**: full fine-tuning with jitter revives the 92 zeroed weights, so the switch trains 154 parameters against the control's 62. C6 shows that dead parameters are an attack surface, not that routing is worse than capacity. **[r4-tamper-power-fix — SUGGESTIVE]**

7. **The patchwork result reproduces to the digit, including the round-3 re-analysis, but its safety framing is measured on the wrong population.** The M2 monitor claim runs on 29 models whose median majority-class rate is 0.962 (62 % emit one class on > 95 % of deploy inputs). "Confidence cannot find the inputs where a routed model has switched worldview" describes a switch the same document's §5 says does not happen in this substrate. Defensible restatement: *confidence does not flag the inputs on which a collapsed model is wrong.* Also: the directional transformer retraction ("controls are MORE patchwork") is a **gate artifact** — the competence gate was applied to the routed arm only and removed exactly 3 models, all 3 patchwork; ungated it is 15/23 vs 19/23 (p = 0.16) and 9/23 vs 15/23 (p = 0.07). The `never` retraction stands (19 counterexamples); the `more` claim does not. **[r4-patchwork-prereg — SUGGESTIVE]**

8. **A pattern worth naming.** Across six audits in rounds 4–5, not one primary statistic failed to reproduce — anchors at max|diff| = 0.0 in every experiment, permutation p-values recomputed on fresh draws, prereg stamps verified by mtime. Every single defect found was in the *interpretive* layer: an asymmetric outcome definition, a gate applied to one arm, a control given a different scoring rule, a null promoted to an equivalence, a diagnostic implemented for one substrate and not the other. **The sprint's measurement discipline is real; its framing discipline is where the errors live.** That is a methods finding and belongs in the paper.

## c) THE LATENT-SWITCH QUESTION

**Is the switch a property of ordinary training rather than routing?** Round 4 said yes. Round 5's answer is: **sometimes, and we can now say exactly when.**

### The defensible sentence

> A standard-trained underdetermined model already contains a post-hoc-selectable worldview switch **iff two conditions hold together**: (i) each data-chosen half is **individually competent** on the training distribution, and (ii) the two cue circuits are **separated along an axis the ablation mask can express**. In an MLP, condition (i) is a width law — both halves clear the 0.9 held-out gate in 5/26/29/30 of 30 seeds at H = 8/16/32/64, and *conditional on competence* the data-chosen mask selects in 2/5, 25/26, 29/29, 30/30. In a from-scratch transformer with a shared residual stream, condition (i) holds (both halves at held-out 1.000, 12/12 in the units-only probe) and condition (ii) **fails**: an axis-aligned head+MLP-unit mask selects **0/30** (Wilson upper 0.114, power 0.87 against a true rate of 0.60), 0/300 per random draw, and 0/12 in an independent re-scoring run. Routing's contribution is exactly the complement of that conjunction.

### Scope, stated as limits

| limit | why it matters |
|---|---|
| The transformer null covers **one organism** and **one mask-granularity family** — masks shared across both layers, axis-aligned over 4 heads and 128 MLP units | The most likely way this null is wrong is "the switch lives in a basis this mask family cannot express". The next experiment is rotated / per-layer subspace ablation, connecting to r4-asymmetry-localizer's signed-eigen localizer |
| It **is** a null about the models, not the instrument | Auditor-added positive control: the same mask-finder on r3 `routed` transformers gives head recovery 1.00, unit recovery 0.71 (chance 0.50), data-chosen mask succeeds **4/4** (planted mask 4/4) |
| Capacity is **excluded** as the transformer's explanation | The units-only mask gives each worldview 64 MLP units per layer — 4× the 16/worldview at which the MLP already scores 29/30 — with both halves at held-out 1.000 and selection margin 0.073 |
| The H = 8 negative ("no hidden switch at round 1's width") has **effective n = 5**, not 30 | Success requires both halves above the 0.9 gate; only 5/30 H = 8 seeds are evaluable, and the mask succeeds in 2 of them. Wilson on 2/5 is [0.076, 0.788]. The published [0.019, 0.213] on 2/30 answers *unconditional selectability*, a different question. **This is the round's one unstated power hole**, and PREREG §9 explicitly committed to reporting it ("if failures are dominated by competence misses, the honest reading is 'the halves are too small'") — the diagnostic was implemented for the transformer and omitted for the MLP |
| Wilson bound on the transformer null is 0.114 | Rates in 0.10–0.40 are not excluded; power was 0.52 at 0.50 and 0.87 at 0.60 |
| Every mask consumes the **cue oracle** | Nothing here is oracle-free localization; r4-asymmetry-localizer is untouched by round 5 |

### The one new safety-relevant observation — and its caveat

Overparameterization appears to make the misaligned worldview resistant to corrective data: with 5 % anti-proxy examples, **19/30 seeds remain worldview-selectable at H = 64 vs 7/30 at H = 16**, and the residual is behaviourally invisible (full deploy accuracy 0.99, 30/30 seeds above 0.8 in both cells) — it takes an ablation to see. **But this is an unlabelled POST-HOC**: the width × f interaction was not preregistered, has no test in `results.json` or `posthoc.json`, yet sits in the confirmatory §4 and is promoted to the implications. Unadjusted Fisher p = 0.0038, and the f = 0 baselines differ (30/30 vs 25/30), so the raw contrast overstates it — relative reductions are 37 % vs 72 %. Report it as a hypothesis for round 6, not as a finding.

### Correction to a headline number

22/30 − 2/30 = **+0.667**, not +0.633. The CI [0.436, 0.800] and Fisher p = 1.4466·10⁻⁷ are exactly right for 22 vs 2; the sibling experiment r5-fair-floor-propagation independently reports +0.667 for the identical comparison. The wrong value appears in the headline, FINDINGS §6 and §7.

## h) THE PAPER, rewritten given rounds 1–5

### Exact title

> **Routing buys a label, not a switch: capacity and basis conditions for post-hoc worldview selection in underdetermined models**

Round 3's title (*Routing is basis-free; localization is not*) and round 4's (*Routing labels a switch that training already built*) are each now too strong in one direction. Round 4's is a **w = 8 MLP statement**. The paper's real contribution is the *conditional*: we can now say, with a measured law and a clean dissociation, when training has already built the switch and when routing is doing the work.

### The claims it can defend, with numbers

**C1 — Underdetermined training already builds two oppositely-committed halves, but only when each half is individually competent.**
Standard-trained models scored under a data-chosen mask (cue sensitivity on held-out **agreeing** data only, no deploy leakage): **51/60 at H = 16**, replicating 15/20 on independently chosen fresh seeds, against random 8/8 splits 1/50 and noise-dimension splits 0/10. The width law: **2/30 → 25/30 → 29/30 → 30/30** at H = 8/16/32/64 (Cochran–Armitage z = 7.97, p = 7.8·10⁻¹⁶). The gate is competence, not selectability: both halves clear the 0.9 held-out bar in **5/26/29/30** of 30 seeds, and conditional on that the mask selects in **2/5, 25/26, 29/29, 30/30**. Pooled across the portfolio: fair floor **88/120 (73 %) at w = 8 vs 12/150 (8 %) at w = 4**, with a cross-cell correlation of **+0.76** between column competence and the fair floor. *Underdetermination does not produce an entangled compromise; it produces a switch nobody labelled — as soon as there is room for two of them.*

**C2 — Routing's effect size is exactly the complement of C1: a width law, not a constant.**
Re-scoring **every** routing-free control in the line under one identical procedure (verified bit-identical to the round-4 rule, max|diff| = 0.0, masks identical 10/10): **+0.150 [0.058, 0.261] at w = 8**; **+0.667 [0.436, 0.801]** (round 1) and **+0.700 [0.471, 0.825]** (round 2) at w = 4; **+0.400** and **+0.567** in the rotated and mixed bases; **+1.000 [0.657, 1.000]** in the transformer. Of 13 cells: 9 survive, 2 shrink, 2 die; the k = 2/w = 8 result (20/20 vs 1/20) is refuted outright and the k-way claims restate to *routing makes an already-present k-way switch reliable* (20/20 vs 11/20 and 9/20). Random splits of the same models succeed 18/4260 (0.42 %), so this is a specific mask, not any mask.

**C3 — In a shared residual stream, no axis-aligned mask finds the switch even though both halves are competent. This is routing's strongest surviving result.**
Round-3 transformer organism, preregistered primary, decision **ABSENT** applied by code: a data-chosen head + MLP-unit mask selects **0/30**; a random balanced mask with **10×** the search freedom 0/30 (0/300 per draw); fixed partition 0/30; distractor mask 0/30. Wilson 95 % upper **0.114**, at a design powered **0.87** against a true rate of 0.60. Independently, a re-training run gives fair floor **0/12** — the only cell in the portfolio where the fair-floor correction moves the number by zero. Not an instrument failure: the same mask-finder recovers a planted partition (head recovery 1.00, unit recovery 0.71 vs chance 0.50) and scores **4/4** on routed transformers. Not capacity: the units-only mask gives each worldview 64 MLP units per layer — 4× the width at which the MLP saturates — with both halves at held-out **1.000** and a selection margin of 0.073. Under FMP both groups land mid-alignment on 9/12 seeds with 79 % passing the competence gate: **competence without commitment**.

**C4 — Localization has an exact obstruction at cue symmetry and is solvable just off it, for orthogonal entanglement.**
Unchanged from round 4 and independently audited SOLID. At exactly equal cue strength |G| = 64 with orbit 2 on the true split (exact enumeration, 16 ratios), so any equivariant localizer must coin-flip; at every other ratio |G| = 16, orbit 1. A localizer reading the **signed spectrum of D = E[xx′|y=1] − E[xx′|y=0]** recovers the true split **40/40** at r = 2.05 and lifts selectability to 38/50 vs 26/50 for ICA on the same seeds (paired McNemar p = 0.017); r\* = 1.15 at N = 800 with (r\*² − 1) ~ N^(−1/2). **Whitening — ICA's first step and most component pipelines' — is precisely what destroys the resource**, and re-pairing whitened components does not repair it (18/40): a pure interaction, and the preregistered falsifier fired. Under general invertible entanglement the localizer degrades to 13/20 (r = 2.05) and 17/20 (r = 3.00) and the claim does not hold.

**C5 — Routing is worth paying for in exactly one deployment regime, and it is a transport result.**
Four regimes at n = 60, paired. **No-retrain:** routing 60/60 is *equivalent* to ordinary training + a no-gradient intervention (59/60; TOST at δ = 0.15 passes, 90 % CI [0.000, 0.050]). **Unknown target, 3 changes:** routing **loses** — 100 fine-tuning steps per change match it 240/240 seed-for-seed at ~60 % of the example-forwards (1.92 M vs 3.20 M), and ordinary-training-plus-mask switches target 234/240 with **zero** gradient steps at half the training cost, dominating at any horizon. **Unlabelled only:** nobody beats chance, by equivariance (only one of six rules meets the δ = 0.15 bound; the rest are bounded within ±0.19–0.24). **Blind** (no cue oracle at deployment): routing wins decisively from **two** labels — 60/60 vs 4/60 — against a fair 200-mask search that saturates near 51–54/60 at m = 32 with a 57/60 existence ceiling, and stays ahead after adding a free label-free competence filter. **Routing does not create the switch; it records the training-time cue oracle in the architecture for an actor who no longer has it.** On the exchange rate: rescored on the matched outcome, routing gives 16/21/25/25/28 against augmentation's 14/16/24/30/30 at m = 1/2/4/8/32 — no significant difference at any m — but augmentation reaches a ceiling routing does not (30/30, deploy alignment 0.96–0.99, from m ≥ 8), so **augmentation is at least as good and probably better once the labels are trainable.** Agreeing examples buy nothing (8/30 vs 25/30, p = 2·10⁻⁵): it is disagreement, not data.

**C6 — It is not a safety property, in four preregistered ways — one of which we corrected against ourselves.**
(i) A masked switch flips at **0 steps, 0 data, 60/60** — structural.
(ii) Under the realistic functional attacker the switch is indistinguishable from a routing-free model of the same live-parameter count, **bounded at δ\* = 0.065 AUC = 36 % of the total 0.178 defense effect**; it does beat that control under the weaker preregistered LoRA attacker (−0.079, p = 0.0014, and −0.080, p = 0.0034 vs the corrected baseline, overturning two round-3 nulls). *We withdraw the round-4 clause that the capacity control is more resistant under the strongest attacker*: full fine-tuning with jitter revives 92 zeroed weights, so the switch trains 154 parameters against the control's 62 — that comparison shows dead parameters are an attack surface, not that routing is worse than capacity. The resistant tail is present in the capacity control at a similar rate (11/60 vs 9/60) and absent from the corrected baseline (0/60, p = 5.9·10⁻⁴), but "at the same rate" is bounded only to [−0.10, +0.17] and remains a suggestion.
(iii) **Corrected against our own earlier claim:** benign fine-tuning does **not** erase the switch. Rounds 3–4 reported 23/23 → 12/23 and 0/30 remaining switchable — both scored under a fixed arbitrary partition. Under a data-chosen mask, **44/60 (73 %, [0.610, 0.829]) of fine-tuned models are still switchable.** What benign fine-tuning destroys is the *labelling*.
(iv) The unselected model is not a hedger (median determinism 0.786 [0.622, 0.912] and 0.951 [0.869, 0.995] in two substrates) but a **confident off-distribution class collapse** (majority-class 0.893 vs 0.550, p = 1.3·10⁻¹⁰, at confidence 0.911, while scoring 1.000 on agreeing data), and **per-input confidence does not flag the inputs on which such a model is wrong** (median AUROC 0.521, 90 % CI [0.477, 0.549], equivalence at the very edge of its ±0.05 margin; model-level triage anti-informative at **0.282** against determinism-matched controls). "Standard training never produces this mode" is dead (transformer controls 19/23), but the stronger "controls are *more* patchwork" must be dropped — it is an artifact of a competence gate applied to the routed arm alone. *Selection is a mandatory deployment step and there is no confidence monitor that catches you skipping it — and ordinary underdetermined models are equally invisible, so this is a limit of confidence monitoring, not a routing liability.*

### What it concedes up front — in the abstract, not a footnote

1. **We got our own effect size wrong twice, in the same way, and found it ourselves both times.** Routing-free floors of 0/30 and 0/20 were produced by scoring the control under an *arbitrary fixed* unit partition. Under a data-chosen mask the pooled floor moves 2/282 → 100/282. The **same** artifact inflated our claim that benign fine-tuning erases the switch (0/30 → 44/60 still switchable). Any ablation-based control must be given the same freedom to choose what to ablate that the method gets for free.
2. **The headline effect size is width-conditional, and the width law itself is post-hoc.** It was read off the confirmatory numbers, not predicted; the pooled w = 4 vs w = 8 comparison mixes cells differing in k and input basis, and w is confounded with total hidden size H = k·w.
3. **The transformer null is one organism and one mask family** — axis-aligned, shared across both layers, over 4 heads and 128 MLP units. It is a null about the models, not the instrument (positive control 4/4), and capacity is excluded — but it is not proof that no basis works. The obvious next control is rotated / per-layer subspace ablation.
4. **The "no latent switch at round 1's width" claim has effective n = 5, not 30.** Only 5/30 H = 8 seeds have two individually competent halves; among those the mask succeeds in 2 (Wilson [0.076, 0.788]). We report the conditional rate, not the unconditional one, for this cell.
5. **The oracle-free localizer is a model-based estimator.** D's diagonality follows from the generative family; the 40/40 is a numerical confirmation of exact algebra, oracle-free per instance but embodying designer knowledge. It covers **orthogonal** entanglement only.
6. **Routing's one winning regime is near-definitional.** The routed arm is trained by occluding cue B for subnet A, so subnet A *is* the cue-A subnet by construction. The empirical content is the size of the blind control's identification problem, not the existence of the win. And in this toy the cues are separate input columns, so the deployment-time interventions routing ties with (input occlusion, sensitivity masks) may not exist in a real model — which arguably makes the blind cell the realistic one.
7. **Toy scale throughout.** ≤ 11-dimensional synthetic inputs, ≤ 1.2 k-parameter MLPs plus one 68.5 k-parameter from-scratch transformer, searched-for equal-cost XOR cues, one deploy distribution. Closed-form column readability is a property of a worldview that *is* a 2-bit XOR.
8. **Provenance, and this project's own failure mode.** Six independent audits across rounds 4–5. **Not one primary statistic failed to reproduce** — anchors at max|diff| = 0.0 everywhere, permutation p-values recomputed on fresh draws, prereg stamps verified by mtime. **Every defect found was interpretive**: an asymmetric outcome definition, a gate applied to one arm, a diagnostic implemented for the substrate where it defends the conclusion and omitted where it would qualify it. Readers should weight our measurements and discount our framings accordingly; we recommend preregistering diagnostics as one-row-per-cell tables so an omitted cell is visible.

### The most likely reviewer objection, and the answer

> **"If routing only wins where the network is too narrow or the basis is wrong, your result is that routing compensates for under-provisioned capacity. Just add width."**

**Partly conceded, and then it hits a hard wall.** Concede the first half without hedging: at w = 8 routing buys +0.150, and at ≥ 16 units per worldview standard training reaches 29–30/30 on its own — at that point routing's contribution is reliability and free labelling, nothing more, and the paper says so in the abstract. The width law is a real deflation of rounds 1–3 and we publish it as such.

But the objection makes a prediction, and the transformer falsifies it. That organism gives each worldview **64 MLP units per layer** — four times the width at which the MLP saturates. Both data-chosen halves score **1.000** on held-out data, so competence is not the binding constraint. And the mask still selects **0/30** (Wilson upper 0.114 at 0.87 power against a rate of 0.60; 0/300 per random draw; 0/12 in an independent re-training run — the only cell in the portfolio where the fair-floor correction moves nothing). The instrument works: the same mask-finder recovers a planted partition at head recovery 1.00 and scores 4/4 on routed transformers. Under FMP both groups pass the competence gate 79 % of the time and still land at deploy alignment ≈ 0.50 on both sides — **competence without commitment**.

So the law is a conjunction, and width satisfies only one conjunct. Adding width buys you the latent switch in an MLP whose cue circuits are already unit-localized; it does not buy you one in a shared residual stream, where the two circuits are not separated along any axis an ablation mask in this family can express. That is precisely the architecture real models have, and it is where routing's effect is +1.000 rather than +0.150.

*Runner-up objection, answered briefly:* **"Your fair floor consumed the cue oracle, so the control got information the method did not."** True, and the two biases run in opposite directions, which we state. The control's mask procedure uses the cue oracle while round 2's and both entangled cells' *method* arms were oracle-free learned localizers — so those corrected effects are **conservative** (their floors are, if anything, too strong). Conversely the routed arms were not themselves re-scored outside two round-4 anchor configurations (`anchor_disjoint_w4`: learned mask 22/30 with 30/30 exact partition recovery; ceiling 60/60), so in the multiway, entangled and LM cells D_new is an **upper bound** on the honest effect. The correction's *direction* is not in question: it is one procedure applied identically, bit-identical to round 4's rule (max|diff| = 0.0, masks identical 10/10), and random splits of the same models succeed 0.42 % of the time.

### The one sentence to open with

> Underdetermined training does not average two incompatible worldviews together — it builds both and picks one by lottery; whether you can select between them afterwards *without* routing depends on two conditions we can now measure, each half being individually competent and the two circuits lying along an axis your mask can express, and gradient routing's real contribution is exactly the complement: near zero where both hold (+0.150), large where capacity is tight (+0.667), total in a shared residual stream (+1.000) — and what it uniquely provides there is not the switch but the label.
